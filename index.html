About

I'm a PhD candidate in physics at The Ohio State University, based in the Department of Astronomy and the Center for Cosmology and AstroParticle Physics (CCAPP). I work with Prof. Anil K. Pradhan and Prof. Sultana N. Nahar as part of the R-Matrix Opacity Project (RMOP).

Research

Opacity is how strongly a hot, dense plasma absorbs light, and it controls how energy gets out of a star. The light you see left the Sun's surface about eight minutes ago, but the energy in it may have spent tens of thousands of years working its way out of the core. Opacity is what makes that trip so slow, and I compute the atomic data behind it.

R-matrix calculations

Large-scale Breit-Pauli R-matrix (BPRM) calculations give photoabsorption cross sections with their full resonance structure and fine structure.

Plasma effects

Electron collisions, ion microfields and thermal motion broaden and smear atomic resonances. I study how this changes cross sections and opacity spectra across temperature and density.

Opacity and equation of state

Building opacities for stellar mixtures and comparing them with equation-of-state results.

Why it matters

The solar opacity problem, helioseismology, and high-energy-density plasma experiments in the lab.
